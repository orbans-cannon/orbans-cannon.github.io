This site is a guide organized by timing model, not a textbook. If you want to fill in the basics of distributed systems first, or look at directions this site deliberately leaves out (for example permissionless consensus), the books and the survey below are reasonable places to start.

**Only the bibliographic details (title, authors, publisher and year) of this page have been checked; the works themselves have not been read closely.** So the page lists only titles and the chapters visible in the books, does not evaluate their content, and does not use them as the source of any claim on this site.

## Textbooks

- Christian Cachin, Rachid Guerraoui, Luís Rodrigues, *Introduction to Reliable and Secure Distributed Programming* (Springer, 2011). It has a chapter of its own on Reliable Broadcast. [doi:10.1007/978-3-642-15260-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-15260-3)
- Hagit Attiya, Jennifer Welch, *Distributed Computing: Fundamentals, Simulations, and Advanced Topics* (Wiley, 2004). [doi:10.1002/0471478210](https://doi.org/10.1002/0471478210)
- Michel Raynal, *Fault-Tolerant Message-Passing Distributed Systems: An Algorithmic Approach* (Springer, 2018). It has chapters on read/write registers and on quorums. [doi:10.1007/978-3-319-94141-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-94141-7)

## Survey

- Shehar Bano, Alberto Sonnino, Mustafa Al-Bassam, Sarah Azouvi, et al., *SoK: Consensus in the Age of Blockchains*, 1st ACM Conference on Advances in Financial Technologies (AFT 2019), pp. 183–198. [doi:10.1145/3318041.3355458](https://doi.org/10.1145/3318041.3355458). This site does not cover consensus on the permissionless side; this is one way in.
